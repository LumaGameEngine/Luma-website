Why isn't the Rust edition open source?
The Rust edition is a commercial product. The C++ edition is free and open source — if you want the source code, use that one. The Rust edition focuses on advanced rendering techniques that are still under active development, and the revenue funds that work.
What does the demo include?
The planned demo is the full engine, running for 30 days from first launch. Every feature is enabled. The only differences are: it expires after 30 days, and any project exports carry a small Luma watermark. No credit card required. The demo isn't available to download yet.
Can I switch plans later?
Yes. You can upgrade from Monthly to One-time at any time, and the remaining balance of your current monthly period is credited against the purchase. Downgrading from One-time to Monthly is not possible.
How are updates delivered?
The Rust edition uses its native launcher. It checks for updates when it starts, downloads the new version in the background, and swaps the running engine on your next launch. You never have to manually reinstall.
What happens if Windows or macOS builds aren't ready yet?
If you purchase a plan, it applies to all platforms. You can start using it on Linux today and switch to Windows or macOS as soon as those builds ship. Your license is per-user, not per-platform.
Is checkout available now?
Not yet. Luma Engine is still under active development, and payments will be enabled when the engine reaches a stable enough state to justify selling access. The demo and paid plans aren't available yet. When they are ready, this page will be updated.
